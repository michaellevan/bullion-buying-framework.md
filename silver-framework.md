# Silver & Gold Buying Framework (Michael LeVan)

## Core Philosophy

Buy liquid, recognizable metal at the best possible price per ounce.

Avoid getting trapped in premium, novelty, or hard-to-resell items.

Always compare a potential purchase against the best available alternatives:
- non-duplicate ATB 5 oz coins near melt
- sovereign silver near spot
- Buffalo rounds at or under spot
- gold at or near spot

If it does not beat those, pass.

## Tier 1 Instant Buys

These are the highest-priority buys.

### ATB 5 oz Silver Coins

Highest priority when all of the following are true:
- non-duplicate coin
- recognizable ATB issue
- priced near melt

#### Current owned ATB coins
- Weir Farm
- American Samoa National Park
- Salt River Bay
- Great Sand Dunes
- Ellis Island
- Harpers Ferry

#### ATB buy rules
- Ideal: **melt + $5**
- Strong buy: **melt + $10 or less**
- Usually avoid: **premium over $40** unless legitimately scarce and still competitive versus other options

At **$73.49 spot**, 5 oz melt is about **$367.45**.

That means:
- Ideal ATB buy: about **$372.45**
- Strong buy ceiling: about **$377.45**

### 1 oz Buffalo Rounds

Preferred generic silver due to high recognizability and easy resale.

#### Buy thresholds
- At or under spot: **excellent / priority buy**
- Small premium over spot: still good if it clearly beats sovereigns
- Around **$77/oz**: usually pass unless market options are poor

### Sovereign Silver

Preferred sovereigns:
- American Silver Eagles
- Canadian Maples
- Britannias

#### Buy rules
- Near spot: **instant-buy territory**
- Small premium over spot: acceptable
- If pricing is not clearly competitive with ATBs or Buffalo rounds, pass

### 1 oz American Gold Eagle

- **At spot** = Tier 1 buy
- Use this as the gold benchmark against all other gold offers

## Tier 2 Good Buys

These are good, but not better than Tier 1.

### Fractional Gold

Acceptable when the premium is likely recoverable on resale.

Preferred types:
- fractional AGE
- fractional Britannia
- fractional Philharmonic
- other recognizable sovereign fractional gold

#### Rules
- Compare everything against **1 oz AGE at spot baseline**
- Accept only if premium is reasonable and resale is likely strong

### Sovereign Silver with Modest Premium

Examples:
- Maples
- Britannias
- imperfect ASEs

Fine to buy when the premium is small and the item remains highly liquid.

### Fairly Priced ATB Quarter Sets

Can be worth considering if pricing is fair, but these are secondary to 5 oz ATBs near melt.

## Situational Buy Rules by Category

### American Silver Eagles (ASEs)

- Best when near spot
- Imperfect ASEs are acceptable
- Do not pay a strong collector premium

### Canadian 1.25 oz Bison

Evaluate strictly on **per-ounce price**.

#### Buy thresholds
- **$75/oz or less** = strong buy
- **$75–77/oz** = acceptable
- **Over $78/oz** = pass

### Recognizable Bars

Examples:
- Sunshine
- Asahi
- other common, liquid refinery bars

#### Rules
- Near spot = acceptable
- Do not pay up just for shape, branding, or packaging

## Default Pass List

### Premium / Collector Silver
- proof coins
- reverse proofs
- slabbed coins
- modern graded premium bullion
- limited mintage hype pieces

### Novelty / Licensed Silver
- Star Wars
- Marvel
- Disney
- colorized pieces
- gilded pieces
- anything bought mainly for theme appeal

### Fractional Silver
- 1 g, 5 g, 10 g silver bars
- 1/4 oz or 1/2 oz silver rounds and coins

Reason: high premium and weaker resale efficiency.

### 40% Silver
- Kennedy halves
- other 40% issues

Reason: weaker demand, weaker liquidity, often trades poorly.

### 90% Junk / Constitutional Silver

Usually pass.

Only consider at a **deep discount** because:
- lower liquidity than straightforward bullion
- more awkward resale
- easy to get distracted by face-value pricing that is not actually competitive

### Vintage / Premium Bars
- Engelhard
- Geiger
- shaped or specialty bars
- collectible refinery pieces

Only buy if they are somehow priced near spot and genuinely beat better alternatives.

### Foreign / Historical Coins
- Spanish reales
- miscellaneous world silver
- historical collector coins

Reason: collector market required, not efficient bullion stacking.

### Generic Silver Around $77/oz

Usually pass unless there is a strong reason it is better than the other available alternatives.

## Deal Structure Rules

### Bundle Trap Rule

If you must buy overpriced items to unlock a better item, pass.

### Mixed Lot Rule

Mixed small lots often hide bad premiums, weak pieces, or undesirable leftovers. Usually pass.

### Cheap but Bad Rule

Cheap silver that is hard to sell is not automatically a good deal.

### No-Duplicate ATB Rule

Do not buy an ATB you already own unless the deal is unusually strong and clearly worthwhile as bullion.

## Decision Framework

### Step 1: Convert to $/oz

Always normalize the price.

Useful conversion:
- **$1 face value of 90% silver = 0.715 oz**

### Step 2: Compare Against Real Baselines

Ask:
1. Does this beat a non-duplicate ATB near melt?
2. Does this beat sovereign silver near spot?
3. Does this beat Buffalo rounds at or under spot?
4. If gold, does this beat or at least reasonably compare to AGE at spot?

If not, pass.

### Step 3: Liquidity Check

Ask:

> Would someone want this tomorrow without needing a long explanation?

- Yes: consider it
- No: pass

### Step 4: Premium Reality Check

Do not pay a premium unless one of these is true:
- it materially improves liquidity
- it is recoverable on resale
- it is still better than your current alternatives

## Final Rule

Do not buy something you do not really want just because it looks like a deal.

A real deal is:
- liquid
- recognizable
- efficiently priced
- easy to resell
- better than your current alternatives

---

## Strategy Summary

### Primary focus
- non-duplicate ATB 5 oz coins near melt
- Buffalo rounds at or under spot
- Maples, Britannias, and other sovereign silver near spot
- occasional ASE near spot
- low-premium, recognizable fractional gold
- 1 oz AGE at spot whenever available

### Usually avoid
- premium distractions
- novelty bullion
- collector traps
- weak-liquidity silver
- expensive generic silver that does not outperform better alternatives

---

## Mindset

You are not buying metal just to own metal.

You are acquiring **liquid, efficient wealth storage**.
